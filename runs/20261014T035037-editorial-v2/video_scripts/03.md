### Scene 2 — Reserve money for repetition
<!-- 03-c2 -->

**Narration**

Before comparing candidates, fix the number of repeat runs. Reserve enough budget to rerun promising changes.

NanoProteinLM’s authors report training every candidate with two seeds and retaining it only when its mean gain exceeds the seed-to-seed spread. That is their protocol, not a universal prescription for how many repeats are enough.

The solar-segmentation report identifies later improvements around one-thousandth of its overlap metric, within single-run variation that its protocol does not estimate. Our interpretation: look for gains larger than run-to-run variation. A positive validation change alone is insufficient reason to adopt an edit.

### Scene 3 — Test whether changes transfer
<!-- 03-c3 -->

**Narration**

Freeze your selected candidate before testing it on fresh tasks. Require evidence of transfer before making a retained edit your default.

In the solar campaigns, Dice was a large, repeatedly retained change on GX10-class hardware, but decreased the overlap score on the 4070 baseline. That does not isolate hardware as the cause: the coding model was confounded with GPU class.

The practical lesson is to test transfer, while keeping the causal explanation open. A change that survives one search has not automatically earned a place in every future experiment.

### Scene 4 — Count the whole comparison
<!-- 03-c4 -->

**Narration**

Budget the entire comparison. The tutorial proposes three development tasks with three attempts each: nine worker trials per evaluation. A baseline plus three candidate evaluations makes thirty-six trials. Comparing two versions on two held-out tasks, three times each, adds twelve.

That totals forty-eight worker trials, before optimizer reasoning, setup, or rehearsals. This is planning arithmetic, not observed expenditure. Include those additional costs when deciding how much search you can afford, and preserve a budget for verification.

### Scene 5 — Preserve a reconstructable record
<!-- 03-c5 -->

**Narration**

Keep failed trials alongside successful ones, with enough versioned evidence to reconstruct each result.

Agora makes this requirement concrete: a verification record should identify the target version, reproduction configuration, evaluator, and reproduced value. A verdict alone is insufficient.

Its authors also say they did not rerun the winning method, and describe missing logs and pinned dependencies. Our recommendation is to look for outside reproductions that supply the reconstruction details, rather than treating a verification label as strong validation by itself.
