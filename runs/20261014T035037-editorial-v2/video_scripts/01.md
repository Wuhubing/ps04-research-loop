## Scene 3 — Keep the scientific goal visible
<!-- claim_id: 01-c2 -->

**Narration**

NanoProteinLM connects search to a scientific proxy, with a person choosing which recipes to scale. Its authors report long-range contact precision increasing from 28.2 to 36.6 percent at the same 48.4 billion-token training budget. Contact precision is a proxy for downstream protein folding. Equal final training tokens do not establish equal total search costs. Keep both qualifications attached to the result: a human selected scale-up candidates, and the reported training budget does not settle the cost of finding them.

## Scene 4 — Define the boundary
<!-- claim_id: 01-c4 -->

**Narration**

The solar-segmentation protocol makes the boundaries concrete. The agent may edit only the training program. It cannot edit the evaluator and never trains on validation or test images. Each campaign is capped at 24 logged experiments, including the baseline. The one-hour allowance applies to an individual training trial, not the entire campaign. For a student planning a small experiment, this is a useful example of specifying the permitted changes, evaluation boundary, and trial budget before starting.

## Scene 6 — Instructions do not eliminate failures
<!-- claim_id: 01-c5 -->

**Narration**

The production study supplies a caution. It reports eleven consecutive wasted iterations before a human intervened. It also reports repeated tests of an already unsuccessful 100-epoch configuration, despite persistent instructions prohibiting it. Recorded constraints did not eliminate repeated failures. These are the authors’ observations from their systems. The study’s component additions are observational, so they do not establish which addition caused an improvement.

[Source: Production study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30541). ## Scene 7 — The student takeaway
<!-- claim_id: 01-c6 -->

**Narration**

Our practical interpretation is to test a bounded loop with an inspectable history and explicit validation gates. NanoProteinLM reports that four of seven changes survived its final ablation study. Selection needs testing too. The supplied evidence comes from author-run studies and a first-party research blog; no independent replication or outside researcher assessment is supplied. Distinct reporting teams are not independent replications of one result. More agents, or more recorded activity, should not be assumed to increase discovery efficiency.
