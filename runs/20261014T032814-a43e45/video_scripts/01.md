## Scene 3 · 01:05–01:35 · Save where an idea can start

<!-- claim_id: 01-c2 -->

**Narration**

AutoMIP addresses another part of the loop: connecting an idea to the saved state needed to execute it.

Its proposed method jointly selects the next research direction and a compatible historical starting state. It restores the associated artifacts and registers the experiment before execution.

The distinction is concrete. Choosing what to try also means choosing which recoverable code and artifacts to build on. This describes the proposed method, rather than establishing general autonomous discovery.

## Scene 4 · 01:35–02:15 · Keep the human scaling gate

<!-- claim_id: 01-c3 -->

**Narration**

NanoProteinLM makes the human role especially visible.

The authors report that sequential search improved long-range contact precision from twenty-eight point two percent to thirty-six point six percent, at the same forty-eight point four billion training-token budget. A human decided which recipes to scale up.

This is a bounded training-recipe result, not fully autonomous biological discovery.

And the budget qualification matters: equal final training-token budgets do not establish equal total search costs. Keep that uncertainty attached to the performance numbers.

**Visual**

Two bars on a shared zero-based axis: **28.2% → 36.6% long-range contact precision, P@L**. Persistent labels: **Author-reported**; **48.4B final training tokens each**; **Total search cost not established as equal**. Diagram: `Sequential search → Human scaling decision → Larger training run`. Exact excerpt: “with a human deciding which recipes to scale up”. Source: [NanoProteinLM, “A better PLM recipe by hill-climbing NanoProteinLM”](https://jojoml.github.io/blog/2026/nanoproteinlm-agenticls/); S3.

## Scene 5 · 02:15–02:55 · Compare against a simple agent

<!-- claim_id: 01-c4 -->
<!-- claim_id: 01-c6 -->

**Narration**

Malena contributes a controlled comparison. The study holds compute resources, time budget and backbone models fixed. In its coding-agent interface experiment, hidden test scores are never returned to the agents.

The authors report that a single long-running agent session with minimal machinery is a strong baseline, and that the standard components they added did not improve on it.

Our interpretation is to benchmark that simple setup before adding orchestration. This does not establish that extra coordination can never help on other tasks.

**Visual**

Two comparison boxes above shared labels: **Same compute · Same time · Same backbone**. Separate locked box: **Interface experiment: hidden test scores withheld**. Exact excerpt: “the hidden test scores are never communicated back to the agents.” Source: [Malena, §§4.1, 5 and conclusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40303); S4. Change badge from **Author-reported** to **Interpretation** for the final two sentences.

## Scene 6 · 02:55–03:35 · Read the budget correctly

<!-- claim_id: 01-c5 -->
<!-- claim_id: 01-c6 -->

**Narration**

The solar-panel segmentation study applies an existing search protocol to three campaigns. Each campaign is capped at twenty-four experiments, including its baseline.

The agent may edit only the training program. A change is retained when the validation metric improves; otherwise it is reverted.

The one-hour training allowance applies to each trial, not the entire campaign.

With the architecture and evaluator frozen, the authors describe this as close to code-level hyperparameter search. That restriction defines what the experiment can establish.

**Visual**

`Edit training program → Trial: 1 hour → Validate → Keep / Revert`. Above: **3 campaigns; each ≤24 experiments, baseline included**. Lock icons on **Architecture** and **Evaluator**. Exact excerpt: “Each campaign is capped at 24 logged experiments, including the baseline.” Source: [segmentation study, §§1–3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10491); S5.

## Scene 7 · 03:35–04:20 · What remains uncertain

<!-- claim_id: 01-c6 -->

**Narration**

Together, these reports shift attention toward the experimental contract: the permitted changes, the budget, the comparison and the evidence retained.

They are separate research accounts, not independent replications of one result. The supplied evidence includes no independent replication or outside researcher assessment of these results.

Agora explicitly says that measuring discovery per unit of compute requires a matched comparison.

For students, our inference is to test a bounded automated search with explicit records and a simple baseline. These reports do not establish that more autonomous coordination produces more discovery per unit of compute.
