### Scene 3 · Make comparisons inspectable · 1:15–1:50

**Narration**

Next, choose tasks that represent your actual work and whose outcomes you can evaluate.

Why Try AI recommends fresh chats, equal access to files and tools, and judgments tied to specific parts of each answer. Flag anything you cannot verify.

These are usable comparison procedures. The author’s preference for one model on a scoped research task does not establish general superiority. Use the procedure to investigate your own task, without assuming the author’s preference will transfer.

**Narration**

### Scene 5 · Review the plan before collecting data · 2:25–3:00
<!-- 03-c5 -->

**Narration**

Documentation should begin before the experiment.

Campus Talks recommends describing study plans before collecting data, sharing code and variable descriptions, and allowing extra planning time.

Applied to agent experiments, our interpretation is to add a prospective review checkpoint: inspect the plan before the run begins. Budget time for that review.

These podcast recommendations are methodological advice, not controlled evidence that this process improves outcomes. They support making the pilot reviewable, without promising a speedup or cost saving.

### Scene 7 · What would justify more delegation? · 3:35–4:20

**Narration**

What evidence should you watch for next?

Our recommendation is repeated performance on unused tasks, accompanied by inspectable code and retained failure records. The tutorial proposes repeated comparisons and warns that better development scores without held-out improvement do not demonstrate transferable improvement.

Do not expand delegation on that basis alone.
